# Barndominium design brief

The shared building: Phase 1 housing for the group during the build, then the workshop and community building owned by the property owners' association. Permitted as a residence from the first application, with septic sized for the Phase 1 occupant count. Source: the Friendstead analysis (Claude Doc tab 1, Entity design and Per-member cost model; vault note "Entity and Ownership Design").

**v6 (24 Oct 2026) reset the brief to the cheapest functional building.** Dry it in, foam it, rough the inside with temporary rooms, live in it while the houses go up, and decide the final fit-out afterwards (guest house plus shop, or whatever the group then wants). Two storeys in the house half so the floor area reaches 3,500 sq ft on a 2,240 sq ft slab. The porch, timber entry gable, vaulted great room and finished wet core of v5 are gone from the base scope; the porch is a later add-on.

## Budget, v6

Model estimate **[M]**, not quotes, Texas 2026 price level, itemised with quantities and unit prices in [ESTIMATE-v6.md](ESTIMATE-v6.md) (generated by `python3 -I notes/barndo-v6-estimate.py`). Anchored on the Cedar's itemised self-build (Iowa 2024) **[S86]**, October 2026 lumber prices **[S118]**, door and slab guides **[S119]** **[S120]**. Member labor is hours, never priced; materials carry 8.25% sales tax.



Dry shell only (site, slab, structure, doors and windows, equipment, permits): about $119,000, $131,000 with contingency. $234,000 is $66 per sq ft of floor and $104 per sq ft of slab. Septic ($25,000, aerobic for 8) is on top and already in the shared pot. v5 carried $256,000 for a 2,800 sq ft finished house and shop; v6 carries $234,000 for 3,500 sq ft of dried-in, foamed, rough space, so the shared pot falls from about $955,000 to $939,000 and the per-household share with six households from $159,000 to $157,000.

Two code items are in the takeoff that a shell quote will not show: a 15-minute thermal barrier over the foam wherever people live (IRC R316.4; an intumescent coating, about $6,500, is cheaper than drywalling the shell), and the shop-to-dwelling separation on the partition (IRC R302.6, 5/8 in type X and a 20-minute door, about $1,000).

**Red iron instead of post-frame.** A 56 x 40 x 20 kit with two framed 12 x 12 openings is about $60,000 to $75,000 in 2026, plus crane and crew erection at $4 to $6 per sq ft ($9,000 to $13,000): roughly $20,000 to $30,000 more than a member-erected post-frame, for about 500 fewer member hours. Get both quotes.

**Member hours.** The Cedar's builder dried in 2,560 sq ft, prepped the slab, prepped for foam and framed the interior in 421 skilled hours. With 14% more skin, the second floor and the partitions, and amateurs at 1.3 to 1.5 times slower, budget 900 to 1,100 member hours for this building, inside the 3,000 shared crew hours in the cost model.

## Open design questions

- [ ] Both shop doors on the west gable (as modelled) or one on the gable and one on the north eave wall for a drive-through bay
- [ ] Which direction the drive arrives from; mirror the shop to the east end if needed
- [ ] Post-frame or red iron: ask for both quotes with the second-floor load and Texas wind speed on the drawings
- [ ] Whether the second floor is in the first permit or added under the same permit once the shell is up (the engineer's post sizing has to assume it either way)
- [ ] Upstairs bath: no plumbing upstairs in Phase 1; a stack above bath 2 is the cheap place if the guest-house plan wants one
- [ ] Window count: twelve small windows is the floor for light and egress; each bunk room upstairs needs an egress window under the residential code
- [ ] Whether the group builds this first as the training project before the houses
- [ ] South porch later: keep the south wall free of utilities so a shed porch can tie in under the eave
- [x] Length: 70 ft v5 with a 25 ft vaulted great room replaced by 56 ft with a second floor, 24 Oct 2026
- [x] Two-storey: previously judged not cheaper for a finished metal building; cheaper here because the second floor is a member-built wood deck inside a shell that already needs the height for 12 ft doors, and the roof and slab are the expensive parts
