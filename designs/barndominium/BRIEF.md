# Barndominium design brief

The shared building: Phase 1 housing for the group during the build, then the workshop and community building owned by the property owners' association. Permitted as a residence from the first application, with septic sized for the Phase 1 occupant count. Source: the Friendstead analysis (Claude Doc tab 1, Entity design and Per-member cost model; vault note "Entity and Ownership Design").

## Open design questions

- [ ] Loft or mezzanine over the bedrooms: with 10 ft walls there is no room for one unless the frame goes to a raised-eave or a clerestory, so this is probably off the table
- [ ] Which walls in the living half are permanent (baths, kitchen) and which come out in Phase 2
- [x] Length: 60 ft (v4) gave a 15 ft great room, too narrow; v5 is 70 ft with a 25 ft great room. The cost model should carry 2,800 sq ft
- [ ] Two-story or loft: judged not cheaper for a metal building (roof and slab are the cheap parts; floor, stairs, taller walls and height work eat the saving); revisit only for bedrooms over the shop if the group wants more space on the same slab
- [ ] Phase 1 needs 4 bunk rooms; v5 has 3 bedrooms, so the shop or the great room takes bunks during the build
- [ ] Does the porch run in front of the shop (as modelled) or stop at the house, saving 20 ft of porch roof
- [ ] Shop on the west end (as modelled) or mirrored to the east, depending on which way the drive comes in
- [ ] Door and window schedule once a frame vendor's bay spacing is known
- [ ] Whether the group builds this first as the training project before the houses
- [ ] Post-frame instead of red iron: the Cedar's shell was erected by one builder in 421 hours with a telehandler, so members could erect a post-frame shell themselves, about $105,000 of bid-priced labor on a house this size; red iron needs a crane and the vendor's crew. Ask for both quotes
