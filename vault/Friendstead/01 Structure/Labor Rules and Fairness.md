# Labor Rules and Fairness

How the hours-only labor design handles differing skill and capacity, and how it stops freeloading and abuse, without putting a price on anyone's hour. These are recommended defaults for the Mutual Construction Labor Agreement, written 25 Oct 2026 for the group to decide and the CPA and attorney to check; not legal or tax advice. Part of [[Friendstead]]. The tax position is in [[Entity and Ownership Design#Labor without a tax bill]], exits and expulsion in [[Governance and Exit]], who may do which trade in [[Member Labor Matrix]].

> [!abstract] The short answer
> **Hours are flat, pooled and verified.** Skill is handled by assignment and training, never by weighting anyone's hour. Freeloading is handled by quarterly pacing, a hold-back of the shared pot that is apportioned by hours delivered at the end, and the breach-and-cure path already in the governance note. Every rule below is tested against one question: does it put a dollar value on an hour, or move value from one household to another? If it does, it is out.
>
> The two obvious "fairness" fixes fail that test. Weighting a skilled member's hour at 1.5 is a relative price record. Letting a busy household buy out hours at a stated rate is a price. Both are dropped.

## Why an hour is never weighted

Three reasons, in order of weight.

1. **It is a price.** The no-barter position rests on there being no stated value for anyone's work. A multiplier says a framer's hour is worth 1.5 of a painter's hour, which is a relative value record, and the shortfall formula then turns it into dollars **[S73]** **[S75]**.
2. **"Similar services."** The barter-exchange regulation excludes "the informal exchange of similar services on a noncommercial basis" (Treas. Reg. § 1.6045-1(a)(4)) **[S73]**. Flat hours from generalists are similar services. Tiered, priced skills are not.
3. **The tested precedent does not weight.** USDA's mutual self-help housing program has run since 1971 on groups of four to ten families who "complete construction work on their homes by an exchange of labor with one another", each family contributing labor on each other's homes toward 65% of the listed tasks, with a substitute allowed only when a family is incapacitated (7 CFR § 1944.403(i), (k)) **[S135]**. Hours are flat, a hired construction supervisor assigns the work, and "no one moves in until all the homes are completed" **[S135]**. That program has produced tens of thousands of homes without pricing an hour.

## Skill and capacity

- **Equalize by assignment, not by credit.** The day's crew lead assigns work from a written qualified-task list kept per person by the superintendent. Skilled members take lead roles, which carry responsibility, layout and planning time, and that planning time is legitimately more hours on the ledger.
- **Train before counting.** Telehandler, scaffolding above 6 ft, roof work, powered framing tools and the spray rig (if the group ever runs one) count only after a short certification the mentor signs. The certification list is an exhibit the managers can extend; the hour rule itself cannot change without 6 of 6.
- **Capacity is not skill.** Same target for every household, with a written accommodation clause: a household that cannot do physical work meets its target through the support tasks that already count. There is no cash substitute by choice; the only cash consequence is the shortfall formula, and it applies the same to everyone.
- **Decide the household factor up front.** One adult or two, same target or a stated ratio for one-adult households (60 to 70% is the range to debate **[J]**). Key it to adults only, never to children, because familial status is protected under fair housing **[S80]**; childcare on work days already counts as hours for whoever does it.
- **Lead roles rotate where they can.** The superintendent's job is quality and safety; the member lead's job is to run the day. Rotating the member lead among the households that are qualified keeps one person from becoming the boss, which matters for the not-an-employer position as well as for friendship ([[Friendstead Risks]], the injury row).

## Freeloaders

The design's weak points are timing and proof: the shared pot is called in the year after closing, the hours arrive over the following two or three years, and a shortfall discovered at the end is discovered when the group has no leverage left. Five additions close that.

**1. Pacing, not just a total.** The target is spread across the build calendar and checked quarterly.

| Quarter result | Response |
|---|---|
| At or above 75% of pace **[J]** | Nothing; the ledger is posted |
| Below 75% of pace for one quarter | Written notice from the managers, a catch-up plan agreed in writing |
| Below 75% for two consecutive quarters, or below 50% in any quarter | Cure period of 60 days **[J]**; the provisional shortfall is booked against that household's hold-back (below) |
| Cure period passes without the plan being met | Material breach of the labor rules: the expulsion path in [[Governance and Exit]], with its own notice and cure, and the put at formula less the top of the discount band |

Disability, a new baby, a job transfer or a divorce are not misconduct, and the agreement should say so: the pacing table still runs, because the other households' houses still slip, but the managers may approve a paused quarter by 4 of 6 with the hours added to later quarters, and the shortfall formula, not expulsion, is the normal outcome for a household that simply cannot deliver.

**2. Verification.** Hours count only when they are logged on the day, on a scheduled task, and signed by the day's crew lead or the superintendent. The ledger is posted to every household weekly. A disputed entry is raised within 14 days or stands. Off-site hours (procurement, bookkeeping, drawings) count up to 20% of the target **[J]** unless the managers pre-approve more. Unscheduled solo work on a shared asset does not count, because nobody saw it and because it is how a well-meaning member builds something the engineer did not draw.

**3. Pooled, never bilateral.** Hours are owed to the group and logged against the project, not against the household whose house was worked on that day. A pairwise ledger ("the Smiths owe the Joneses 40 hours") is an exchange of services between two people; a pooled one is a club. The schedule, not the ledger, decides whose house gets worked on.

**4. Build sequence.** The household whose house comes last is the one that fears the others fading, and that fear is the usual cause of a group breaking up. The mutual self-help rule is the strong version: nobody moves in until every house is complete **[S135]**. Softer versions that keep most of the effect: all six shells dried in before any interior starts; the sequence drawn by lot and published before the first slab; the hour commitment running to the last final inspection, not to each household's own certificate of occupancy; and the barndominium bunk rooms available to any household whose house is not yet habitable, so nobody is paying rent while waiting.

**5. A cash vehicle for the shortfall.** A lien on a lot the household lives on is weak in Texas once the homestead attaches, even with the § 53.254 formalities ([[Friendstead Risks]], the lien and homestead row). So the shortfall formula needs money that is still in the LLC when the hours are counted. Two ways, either or both **[J]**:

- **Hold back the last call.** The final 15 to 20% of the shared pot (roughly $25,000 to $30,000 per household at six) is not called on the ordinary schedule. It is called at the end of the build and apportioned by hours delivered, in brackets rather than per hour: a household at 90% or more of target pays one sixth; at 75 to 90% it pays one sixth plus a quarter; at 50 to 75% one sixth plus a half; below 50% double, with the others' shares falling to match. The brackets are the formula the term sheet has been missing. They are a larger share of a common cost, not a bigger interest in the LLC.
- **A performance deposit.** Each household posts a deposit into the LLC at closing, applied to its share of that last call. It is simpler to enforce than a lien and it is the same money, just earlier.

Either way the adjustment is settled at the last final inspection, or from sale proceeds if a household sells before then, which replaces the "settled at deed" wording in the risk register (the lots deed at plat, before most of the hours exist).

## Quality, safety and abuse of shared things

- **Hours of damage do not count.** Rework caused by a member ignoring the lead's instructions or the drawings is not credited, and the hours spent fixing it are credited to whoever fixes it.
- **Safety strikes.** A first violation removes the member from the crew for the day. A second removes them from powered-equipment and height tasks for 90 days. A third is a material breach. The superintendent and the insurer's rules decide what a violation is; the agreement adopts the written safety rules by reference ([[Friendstead Risks]], the injury row).
- **Shared assets.** A tool checkout log in the shop; each house's materials bought on that house's own construction draw, never from the shared account; a small shared-consumables fund (screws, blades, fuel) replenished by equal cash; shop rules and releases; the barndominium occupancy license with house rules, a utility split and a departure clause.
- **Conduct.** A respect and harassment clause with the same notice, cure and hearing steps the governance note uses for everything else, a named person outside the managers to receive a complaint, and mediation as the second step. A member who treats the others as labor is a governance problem before it is a labor problem; one vote per household, annual manager elections and the dispute ladder are the answer.

## Tax guardrails for anything the group adds

Checked against the regulation text; the CPA sign-off already in [[Term Sheet Decisions]] should cover these specifically.

- No dollar rate per hour anywhere, including the shortfall formula: write it as brackets of the shared-pot share.
- Membership interests, capital accounts and lot assignments stay equal regardless of hours. A capital interest received for services is compensation income under Treas. Reg. § 1.721-1(b)(1) **[S75]**, so the hours-adjusted call must change what a household pays toward a common cost, never what it owns.
- No skill multipliers, no bilateral ledgers, no option to buy out hours at a rate, no refund of hours on exit.
- No wage-like features: no set shifts imposed by a boss, no discipline framed as employment discipline, no pay stubs or rates in the safety rules. Members contribute hours as owners ([[Friendstead Risks]], the injury row).
- Nothing that keys the target or the tasks to children, religion, national origin or any other protected class **[S80]**.

## What the Mutual Construction Labor Agreement must contain

An exhibit to the company agreement, amended only by 6 of 6 for the hour rule and the formula, by 4 of 6 for the task and certification lists.

1. Parties and status: households contribute hours as owners of the LLC, not under a contract of hire; no wages, no rate.
2. Hour target per household, the household factor, and the calendar it is paced against.
3. Tasks that count, the off-site cap, and the tasks reserved to licensed trades or to each owner on their own home.
4. The qualified-task list, certifications, and who signs them.
5. Ledger rules: logging on the day, lead's sign-off, weekly posting, 14-day dispute window, pooled accounting.
6. Pacing table, notice, catch-up plans, paused quarters, cure periods.
7. The hold-back or deposit, the brackets, and when it is settled.
8. Build sequence rule and the lot draw.
9. Safety rules by reference, the strike system, equipment and height rules.
10. Shop, tool, materials and consumables rules; the barndominium occupancy license by reference.
11. Conduct clause and complaint route.
12. Warranty disclaimers and mutual releases for member work ([[Friendstead Risks]], construction-defect row).
13. Insurance program by reference: occupational accident or workers' comp through the LLC, general liability, builder's risk.
14. What happens to hours on exit, divorce, death and replacement: never paid out; a replacement household inherits the departing household's remaining target from the date of admission.
