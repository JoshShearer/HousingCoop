# Term Sheet Decisions

Settle the term sheet with the group first, then spend money on professionals in this order: engineer, insurance broker, attorney, lender. Part of [[Friendstead]].

## Before sharing with friends

Share it as a cost-sharing project among people who will all do the work, not as an investment, and mark every number as a draft estimate.

- **Framing.** Interests in a manager-managed LLC sold to friends for cash can be securities under the Securities Act of 1933 and the Texas Securities Act ([Tex. Gov't Code ch. 4001 et seq.](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.4001.htm)). The project stays outside that when every member actively runs it (Williamson v. Tucker, 5th Cir. 1981). Describe it as sharing costs and labor, never quote a return, keep it to people you already know, and have counsel put Rule 506(b) and Texas limited-offering language in the subscription agreement.
- **Labels.** All figures are models, several inputs are unverified, and the building estimates are not quotes. Keep those flags on the shared copy.
- **Pre-formation agreement.** A two-page cost-sharing letter before the LLC exists: who pays the engineer, attorney, option fee and test well; what happens to that money if the project dies; money held in an attorney's trust account or a dedicated LLC account, never a personal one.
- **The decisions.** Send friends the checklist below rather than conclusions, so the group shapes the answers.

## Next steps

1. Close the remaining items in [[Friendstead Open Questions]].
2. Hold one meeting on the term sheet below and record each decision in [[Friendstead Decision Log]]. Aim for 10 to 15 pages.
3. Engage a civil engineer for a one-day review of two or three candidate tracts: lots, septic envelopes, well sites, road and drainage. Budget $3,000 to $6,000 per tract **[J]**.
4. Get an insurance broker's written program for member labor before anyone picks up a tool.
5. Interview two Texas real-estate attorneys who have done small subdivisions and POAs in the target county. Hand them the term sheet and the Claude Doc.
6. Confirm land-loan terms for an LLC borrower with a Farm Credit lender and one portfolio bank, including partial releases as lots are deeded.
7. Make every purchase offer contingent on a 90 to 120 day feasibility period with the engineer's lot map as the exit condition.
8. Have a CPA confirm the hours-only labor design and the in-kind homesite distribution before the term sheet goes to counsel.

## Decisions to make before counsel drafts

- [ ] Household count and the minimum count below which the project does not proceed
- [ ] Six lots or seven, with the seventh sold to fund the road; seller financing as a term of every offer; tract scoring for existing improvements (see [[Further Cost Levers]])
- [ ] Target counties, and whether the 10-acre no-plat path is on the table
- [ ] Cash each household can contribute at closing, and the capital-call ladder
- [ ] Hour target per household (1,500 or 3,000 hours), the household factor for one-adult households, the tasks that count, quarterly pacing, and the hold-back of the last 15 to 20% of the shared pot apportioned by hours in brackets (hours only, no dollar price, no skill weighting; see [[Labor Rules and Fairness]] and [[Entity and Ownership Design#Labor without a tax bill]])
- [ ] Build sequence rule (all shells before any interior, lot draw, commitment to the last final inspection) and the ledger, safety-strike, shared-asset and conduct rules for the labor exhibit ([[Labor Rules and Fairness]])
- [ ] Build system: stick, SIP, steel kit or barndominium, and whether households may differ
- [ ] Home size and finish range, and whether upgrades are individually funded
- [ ] Barndominium scope, permitted use and who owns it in each phase
- [ ] Covenants state affirmatively that solar, storage, rainwater systems, composting, drip irrigation and drought-resistant landscaping are permitted (Prop. Code §§ 202.007, 202.010 void bans anyway), and a board resolution allows the association's own solar on the barndominium, which the common-property exception would otherwise let it refuse (see [[Energy Incentives]])
- [ ] Individual wells or a central system
- [ ] Common tract size: 10 acres (bees) or 15 acres (grazing, wildlife) — see [[Agricultural Valuation]]
- [ ] Voting thresholds: majority, two-thirds, unanimous (recommended 4, 5, 6 of 6 by subject: [[Governance and Exit#Who decides what before the deed]])
- [ ] Parcel allocation method and the equalization baseline
- [ ] Exit formula pre-plat, buyout timing, and the right of first refusal window ([[Governance and Exit#Leaving at each moment]])
- [ ] Rental, short-term rental, commercial use and animal rules for the declaration ([[Governance and Exit#The HOA rules]])
- [ ] Dispute ladder: mediation, arbitration, buy-sell ([[Governance and Exit#Deadlock]])
- [ ] Who the member is: one spouse, both, or a trust; spousal joinder on the company agreement and buy-sell ([[Governance and Exit#Divorce and death]])
- [ ] Personal guarantees on the land loan: pro rata or joint and several, and the contribution agreement among guarantors
- [ ] Minerals: buy them, record a surface-use waiver, or walk
- [ ] Homesite transfer as an in-kind distribution on plat, never a purchase contract
- [ ] Phase 1 occupancy terms for the barndominium: license or lease, house rules, utilities, departure
- [ ] Warranty disclaimers and mutual releases for member work on each other's homes
- [ ] Admitting a later household: price at cost plus carrying costs or at market, and by what vote; admission criteria written and objective, right of first refusal matches price only, no buyer-approval discretion and no stated preference in covenants or marketing (fair housing, [S80])
- [ ] Not an employer: company agreement, labor exhibit, safety rules and releases all say members contribute hours as owners, not under a contract of hire; no wages, no rate, no supervision as a boss; trades are independent contractors ([S78])
- [ ] Pre-formation cost sharing and where money sits before the LLC exists
- [ ] Insurance program in three layers: LLC, POA and household, with the shop release form
