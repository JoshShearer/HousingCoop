# Governance and Exit

Who decides what, how a household leaves at each stage, what a departing spouse or an estate gets, and how much say the others have over who replaces them. The positions here are recommended defaults for the term sheet, not legal advice; the attorney drafts the company agreement and the declaration from them. Statutes are cited by section with the official text linked in [[Friendstead Sources]] (**[S115]** Business Organizations Code ch. 101, **[S116]** Property Code ch. 209).

> [!abstract] The short answer
> **Two regimes, one calendar.** Until a lot is deeded, a household holds an LLC membership interest and the company agreement governs everything: Texas default law lets nobody withdraw or be expelled and makes a departing spouse a silent assignee, so the agreement must say what we actually want. After the deed, the lot is the household's real property. The group keeps a recorded right of first refusal, the covenants and the assessment lien. It does not get a vote on the buyer.
>
> With six households the vote arithmetic is unusual: a simple majority and two-thirds are both four of six, and the statutory 67% for amending a declaration is five of six. The ladder should be written as 4, 5 and 6, not as percentages.

## Two regimes, one calendar

| Phase | What a household holds | Who decides | Governing document | How a household leaves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Term sheet signed, land not yet closed (months 0 to 6) | A refundable deposit and a seat | All signers, informally; the term sheet is non-binding except deposits and confidentiality | Term sheet, deposit escrow | Walk away; deposit back less that household's share of feasibility costs already spent |
| 1. Land in the LLC through plat (months 6 to 18) | A membership interest with a capital account and an assigned future lot | Managers day to day; members by the thresholds below | Company agreement | Formula buyout below, or assignment of the interest to an approved replacement |
| 2. Lots deeded, homes under construction (months 18 to 48) | Fee-simple lot plus a membership interest in the LLC for the shared work still open | Members for shared work; the household alone for its own lot and house | Company agreement plus recorded declaration | Sell the lot subject to the declaration and the right of first refusal; the LLC interest goes with the lot |
| 3. Completed community (month 48 on) | Fee-simple lot and home; one vote in the POA | POA board of three, elected by the owners; owners on budget, amendments and board seats | Declaration, bylaws, Chapter 209 | Sell to anyone at any price; 45-day price-match right in the group |

The LLC is dissolved, or shrinks to a shell, when the last shared item is finished and the common tract and barndominium pass to the POA ([[Entity and Ownership Design#The five pieces]]).

## Who decides what before the deed

The Business Organizations Code leaves almost everything to the company agreement (§ 101.052: the agreement governs relations among members, managers and assignees, and may waive or modify most of the title's provisions). The defaults it supplies when the agreement is silent are thin and wrong for a group of friends: a majority of managers for anything outside the ordinary course (§ 101.356), unanimous consent to admit a new member (§ 101.105), no withdrawal and no expulsion at all (§ 101.107).

Recommended ladder, one vote per household regardless of capital or hours:

| Decision | Who | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Spending within the approved budget, scheduling, hiring a sub inside a budget line, signing permits | Managers (2 or 3, elected annually) | Manager majority |
| Annual budget and schedule, electing managers, work-crew rules, choosing contractors above $25,000 **[J]** | Members | 4 of 6 |
| A capital call beyond the ladder already in the agreement, spending the contingency, borrowing or pledging the land, changing the plat layout, admitting a replacement household, settling a dispute with a member, buying out a member at anything other than the formula | Members | 5 of 6 |
| Amending the company agreement, changing a household's lot assignment, changing the hours rule, any change that treats households unequally, dissolving before plat | Members | 6 of 6 |

Three reasons for this shape. A four-vote threshold lets a bare majority commit the minority's money, so money decisions sit at five. A unanimity requirement on anything routine hands every household a veto, which is how friend groups freeze, so unanimity is reserved for changes to the deal itself. And five of six means one household can be outvoted on admitting its replacement but cannot be ganged up on by a bare four.

## Leaving at each moment

### Before the land closes

Deposits sit in escrow. A household that withdraws gets its deposit back less its share of what the group has spent on feasibility (survey, engineer's lot map, title, legal). Nothing else is owed either way. The term sheet should say so in one sentence so nobody later argues that it was a binding promise to buy a lot ([[Term Sheet Decisions#Before sharing with friends]]).

### Before plat, land in the LLC

Texas default law is the opposite of what the departing household expects. A member may not withdraw or be expelled (§ 101.107), and may only assign the economic interest; the assignee gets the money rights but no vote, no information rights and no membership (§ 101.108). Without an agreement clause, a household that wants out is stuck holding an illiquid interest in a land-development LLC, and the remaining five are stuck with a passive co-owner. The agreement overrides both halves:

- **Put at formula.** A household may require the LLC to buy its interest at the cash it has contributed, less a discount of 15 to 25% **[J]**, paid over 24 months, subordinated to the land loan. The discount is the price of leaving the others short-handed with interest running, and it is what stops the put being used tactically.
- **Hours delivered are not paid out.** Member labor is counted in hours only ([[Entity and Ownership Design#Labor without a tax bill]]). A departing household's hours bought it a place in the build it is now leaving; paying them out would give each hour a dollar value and collapse the no-barter position for everyone. The agreement says this in plain words so the departing household is not surprised. The group as a whole loses those hours, which is part of what the discount offsets.
- **Expulsion for default only.** A household that misses a capital call runs the ladder in [[Friendstead Risks]] (cure period, carry at interest by the others, then forced buyout at formula less the top of the discount band). Expulsion for any other reason, including personality, is not available: the agreement names the capital-call default and a material breach of the labor rules as the only triggers, each with notice and a cure period. What counts as a material breach, and the pacing, notice and cure steps that come before it, are in [[Labor Rules and Fairness]].
- **Replacement first, buyout second.** The departing household may bring a replacement who buys the interest at cost plus carrying costs; the LLC pays the formula only if no replacement is admitted within 90 days **[J]**. See "The replacement household" below for the vote.

### After the deed, before the house is finished

The household now owns a lot, probably with a foundation or a shell on it, and still owes its share of the shared work and any remaining capital schedule. Both obligations are secured: assessments by the declaration's lien, remaining LLC contributions by a recorded lien on the lot in the LLC's favour ([[Friendstead Risks]], the lien and homestead row). The household may sell. The buyer takes the lot subject to the declaration and steps into the LLC interest that runs with it; the group has its 45-day match right at the contract price. The departing household's open hours obligation ends with its membership, and the remaining five absorb that work or the buyer takes it on. There is no vote on the buyer.

### After completion

The home is fee simple and homestead. It can be sold to anyone, at any time, for any price. What the group keeps:

- the recorded right of first refusal, 45 days to match the price and terms of a bona fide third-party contract;
- the covenants, which bind every later owner;
- the assessment lien;
- nothing else.

What the group cannot have: a right to approve or interview the buyer, a residency or "like-minded" requirement, a right to set the price, or a preference stated anywhere for any kind of household. A consent requirement is an unreasonable restraint on alienation and will not survive; any screening of buyers, formal or informal, is a fair-housing exposure for the association and for each owner who takes part (42 U.S.C. § 3604, Tex. Prop. Code § 301.021 **[S80]**). The honest statement to friends is that after completion the community is held together by the covenants and by the people who choose to stay, not by control over who buys in.

## Divorce and death

**Decide who the member is first.** Three choices, and the agreement must pick one: one named spouse as member with a signed spousal consent; both spouses together as a single membership unit with one vote; or a revocable trust. Both spouses jointly as one unit is the cleanest, because then the divorce court is dividing an interest that already names both of them and the vote cannot split. Whichever is chosen, every spouse signs a joinder to the company agreement and the buy-sell terms at formation, because the interest is community property unless it can be traced to separate funds (Tex. Fam. Code § 3.002 **[S71]**), regardless of whose name is on it.

**Divorce before the deed.** On divorce the member's spouse, to the extent of their interest, becomes an assignee (§ 101.1115(a)(1)): money rights, no vote. The agreement adds a buy-sell trigger: when a decree awards any part of the interest to a non-member, the member spouse has 60 days to buy that part at formula, then the LLC, then the other members pro rata. The household keeps its seat if one spouse stays and can carry the capital schedule; if neither can, it is the ordinary put at formula.

**Death before the deed.** The surviving spouse, heirs and the personal representative are assignees (§ 101.1115(a)(2)). Recommended: the surviving spouse may elect within 90 days to become the member on signing the agreement; otherwise the LLC buys at formula with no discount, paid over 24 months, since nobody chose to leave. Key-person life cover on each member for the remaining capital schedule is cheap at these ages **[J]** and worth putting in the term sheet.

**Divorce three years after completion.** The lot and home are real property, almost certainly community property, and the divorce court divides them. The usual outcomes are that one spouse keeps the home and refinances the other out, or the home is sold. If one spouse keeps it, nothing changes for the group: that person is already an owner, the transfer is exempt from the right of first refusal, and no vote is involved. If the home is sold, it sells at market to whoever contracts for it; the group has 45 days to match. The declaration lists the exempt transfers (to a spouse or former spouse under a decree, to descendants, to a trust for the owner's family, to a lender by foreclosure) so a divorce or an estate is never blocked by the match right.

**Death after completion.** Heirs take the lot subject to the declaration. The match right applies when the estate sells, not when it distributes.

## The replacement household

**Before plat.** Admitting a member needs the approval of all members unless the agreement says otherwise (§ 101.105). Recommended: 5 of 6, with written objective criteria so that a refusal is defensible and the departing household cannot be held hostage by one vote. The criteria are financial and contractual only: proof of funds for the remaining capital schedule, a credit and background check at the same standard a lender would apply, signature on the company agreement, the labor rules and the spousal joinder, and attendance at one work weekend before the vote. No criteria about who the people are. Price is cost plus carrying costs through the date of admission, or market by a 5 of 6 vote once the plat is approved and the lots have a market value ([[Term Sheet Decisions]]).

**After the deed.** No vote and no approval. The only lever is the right of first refusal, and a match right is only control if someone can fund the match. Recommended: the right is held by the POA, which may assign it to any one or more owners within the 45 days; there is no obligation on anyone to fund it. In practice it works when a member's family or a friend on the waiting list wants the lot, and otherwise it is a 45-day delay that tells the group who is coming. Say that plainly to friends rather than presenting the match right as a gate.

**A waiting list is the real tool.** A standing list of households who have attended a work weekend and read the declaration gives a seller a buyer who already knows the deal, and gives the group its match. It needs no covenant and no vote.

## Deadlock

| Step | Before the deed | After completion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Manager decision, or member vote at the threshold above | Board decision, open meeting with notice (§ 209.0051) |
| 2 | 30-day mediation with a named mediator (Austin Dispute Resolution Center or an agreed attorney-mediator), cost shared | Same |
| 3 | Binding arbitration for money disputes under $250,000 **[J]**; court for title and injunctive matters | Enforcement only after written notice by certified mail with a cure period and the right to a hearing (§ 209.006) |
| 4 | Shotgun buy-sell between the deadlocked households, usable only after step 2 | Owner vote to replace the board at the next election (§ 209.00593) |
| 5 | Sale of the tract and dissolution by 5 of 6 | Amendment of the declaration by 5 of 6 (§ 209.0041(h)) |

A shotgun clause among six people with unequal savings favours the richer household, so it is a last resort and only after mediation. Before plat the real deadlock-breaker is the sale clause: if five households want to sell the tract and wind up, they can.

## The HOA rules

There are no rules yet. The declaration has not been drafted; this is the list of what it will contain and the recommended position on each item, for the attorney to draft from.

| Covenant | Recommended position |
|---|---|
| Use | Single-family residential; home occupation without client traffic or signage; the barndominium zone for workshop, storage and common use |
| Leasing | Long-term leases of 12 months or more allowed with a copy to the board; short-term rental named and prohibited in terms (a generic "residential use only" clause does not stop it: Tarr v. Timberwood Park, [[Friendstead Risks]]) |
| Commercial | None beyond home occupation; no retail, no kennels, no events for pay |
| Animals | Household pets; livestock and poultry within the written agricultural plan ([[Agricultural Valuation]]); no commercial operations |
| Building envelope | Setbacks from lot lines and the road; one dwelling and one accessory building per lot; impervious cover capped at 20% of the lot, which also keeps a western tract under the Edwards rules threshold ([[Austin Land and Home Market#Rules and water by county]]) |
| Design review | Light: new structures and exterior changes visible from the road only, against written objective standards, deemed approved after 30 days of silence |
| Water and septic | Each lot maintains its own well or connection and its OSSF; no sharing of a well across lots without a recorded easement and the district's sign-off |
| Common tract | Road, shared well or water system, barndominium, open land; maintenance and reserve assessments; a reserve study every five years |
| Assessments | Annual budget by the board, approved by the owners; special assessments above 25% of the annual budget by 5 of 6; late charges and collection as Chapter 209 allows; lien recorded |
| Right of first refusal | 45 days to match a bona fide third-party contract; held by the POA, assignable to owners; exempt transfers listed; expires on its own terms after 30 years unless renewed |
| Amendment | 5 of 6 owners; the statutory default is 67% of the votes (§ 209.0041(h)), which with six lots is five, since four is 66.7% |
| Development period | The LLC as declarant, ending at the earlier of the last lot deeded or five years; the amendment statute does not apply during the development period (§ 209.0041(d)), so the period should be short and the declarant's unilateral powers listed and narrow |

## What Chapter 209 requires of a six-lot association

Chapter 209 applies to a residential subdivision whose owners are required to join an association that can assess them, however small. The procedural duties the board must follow from day one:

| Duty | Section | What it means for six households |
|---|---|---|
| Board meetings open to owners, with notice | § 209.0051 | No decisions over dinner that bind the association; a notice, an agenda and a record |
| Board elected by owners; vacancies appointed only for the remainder of the term | § 209.00593 | Three seats, staggered terms, no self-perpetuating board |
| Notice of an election or vote 10 to 60 days before | § 209.0056 | Dates for the annual meeting and any amendment vote fixed in advance |
| Voting in person, by proxy, by absentee or electronic ballot; quorum rules | § 209.00592 | An email vote is fine if the bylaws provide for it |
| Certified-mail notice, a cure period and a hearing before a fine, a suit or a suspension of common-area rights | § 209.006 | No fines by text message; the friend who parks the trailer wrong gets a letter and a chance to fix it |
| Declaration amended by 67% unless the declaration sets a lower figure | § 209.0041 | Five of six |

None of this is heavy, but it has to be done, and someone has to be secretary. Budget a few hundred dollars a year for the association's own filings and insurance ([[Per-Member Cost Model]]).

## For the term sheet

These are the items in [[Term Sheet Decisions]] this note answers with a recommended default:

- Voting thresholds: 4, 5 and 6 of 6 by subject, one vote per household.
- Exit before plat: put at cash contributed less 15 to 25%, over 24 months, hours not paid out; replacement first, 90 days.
- Expulsion: capital-call default and material labor-rule breach only, with cure periods.
- Labor fairness: flat hours with no skill weighting, quarterly pacing, a held-back final call apportioned by hours in brackets, and the labor-exhibit contents ([[Labor Rules and Fairness]]).
- Who the member is: both spouses as one unit, one vote, joinder signed at formation.
- Divorce and death triggers: member spouse, then LLC, then members; surviving spouse may elect in; no discount on death.
- Admission of a replacement: 5 of 6 on objective financial criteria.
- Right of first refusal: POA-held, 45 days, assignable, exempt transfers, no funding obligation, no buyer approval.
- Dispute ladder: vote, mediation, arbitration, shotgun, sale.
- Declaration contents as in the table above; development period short.
