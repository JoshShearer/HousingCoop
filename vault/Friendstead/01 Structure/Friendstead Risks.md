# Friendstead Risks

The four risks that can sink the project are feasibility, water, a member running out of money or labor, and an injury on a work crew. Each has a known contractual answer; the term sheet should settle them before counsel drafts. Part of [[Friendstead]]. Next steps in [[Term Sheet Decisions]].

| Risk | Likelihood | Cost if it lands | Predetermined response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tract cannot be platted into the planned lots | High without a feasibility period | Loss of earnest money and engineering fees, or a dead project after closing | 90 to 120 day feasibility contingency; engineer's preliminary lot, septic and road map before the option expires; walk-away threshold in the LLC agreement |
| Water: dry or low-yield wells, groundwater district limits, or a central system crossing the public-water-system line | Medium east, high in the Hill Country | $20,000 to $30,000 per failed well; permanent operator duties | Test well during feasibility; individual wells by default; written rule that any central system stays under 15 connections and 25 residents |
| Member cannot meet a capital call | High over 3 years with six households | Project stalls, or the others carry the shortfall | Ladder: 30-day cure, member loan at a set rate, dilution, then buyout at formula value; no new land spending until the call is funded |
| Member cannot deliver labor (disability, transfer, divorce, new baby, loss of interest) | Near certain for at least one household | Others' homes slip; resentment | Hour target in the labor agreement; a shortfall raises that household's cash share of shared development by a set formula, settled at deed or from sale proceeds; no dollar price on hours and no payment between households |
| Injury on a member work crew | Medium over 10,000 crew hours | Medical costs, a lawsuit between friends, loss of the project | Occupational accident or workers' comp through the LLC, general liability, builder's risk, written safety rules; broker engaged before the first work day |
| LLC holds title to a home a member is living in | Certain in Phase 1 | No homestead exemption or creditor protection; no conventional mortgage; lender refuses owner-as-contractor on homestead | Deed lots as soon as the plat records; builder of record for each home; homestead filed the year of the deed |
| Cost overrun of 30% on shared work | Medium | $35,000 more per household | 10% contingency built in; two-thirds vote for spending above budget; equalization credits if one lot's site work costs more |
| Group deadlock or breakdown | Medium | Project freezes with land loan interest running | Mediation, then arbitration, then shotgun buy-sell; liquidation waterfall agreed in advance |
| A member wants out before plat | Medium | Illiquid interest, dispute over value | Formula value at a 15 to 25% discount pre-plat, paid over 24 months, with a right of first refusal |
| Resale to an outsider after completion | Certain within 10 years | Community character changes | Recorded right of first refusal with a 45-day match window; POA covenants on rentals, commercial use and animals; no consent requirement that could void a sale |
| Barndominium permitted as a workshop but occupied as housing | High if not planned | Stop-work order, septic failure, uninsured loss | Permit it as residential from the first application; size the septic for occupants |
| Lien and homestead formalities ignored when the LLC advances money for a member's house | Medium | Unsecured advances; unenforceable liens | Written contract before any work or materials, signed by both spouses where a homestead is involved, Prop. Code § 53.254 |
| Membership pitched as an investment | Low if handled | Rescission rights and penalties under the Texas Securities Act (Tex. Gov't Code ch. 4001 et seq., investment contracts at § 4001.068 **[S70]**) and the Securities Act of 1933 | Every member actively runs the project (Williamson v. Tucker, 5th Cir. 1981); no projected returns, no outsiders, no public pitch; subscription agreement with Rule 506(b) and Texas limited-offering language |
| Severed minerals or an existing oil and gas lease on the tract | High in Caldwell, medium elsewhere | Drilling, roads and pipelines on the surface with no veto; the mineral estate is dominant (Getty Oil v. Jones, Tex. 1971) | Mineral and lease title review in the feasibility period; buy the minerals or record a surface-use waiver; walk if neither is available |
| Personal guarantees on the land loan | Certain | One member answers for the whole loan if the others cannot | Pro rata guarantees where the lender allows; contribution agreement among guarantors; lot release schedule written into the loan |
| Construction-defect claims between members | Medium | Implied warranties of habitability and workmanship on a home the LLC builds and deeds (Humber v. Morton, Tex. 1968) **[S76]**; claims under the Residential Construction Liability Act, Prop. Code ch. 27, whose contractor definition covers anyone who builds and sells a new residence **[S72]** | Deeds and the labor agreement carry clear, conspicuous disclaimers (Centex Homes v. Buecher, Tex. 2002) and mutual releases for member work; licensed trades keep their own warranties |
| A household's pre-deed right treated as a contract for deed | Medium | Residential executory-contract duties and penalties under Prop. Code §§ 5.061 to 5.085 | The homesite passes as an in-kind distribution on plat under the company agreement, not under a purchase contract; no installment payments tied to a specific lot before plat; a contract that delivers the deed within 180 days is exempt under § 5.062(c) **[S69]** |
| Six households living in an LLC-owned barndominium | Certain in Phase 1 | Residential tenancy duties under Prop. Code ch. 92; builder's risk policies exclude occupied buildings | Short occupancy license or lease, house rules, utility split and departure clause; dwelling or commercial property policy on the barndominium |
| Spouse claims half a membership interest | Medium on divorce or death | Community property (Fam. Code § 3.002 **[S71]**) puts an ex-spouse or an estate into the LLC | Decide who the member is (one spouse, both, or a trust); spousal joinder on the company agreement and buy-sell; buyout on divorce or death |
| A generic residential-use covenant fails to stop short-term rentals | Certain if left generic | Tarr v. Timberwood Park (Tex. 2018) read residential use to allow short-term rentals | Declaration names short-term rental, a minimum lease term and commercial use expressly |
| Member sued personally for an accident on the road, in the shop or on the common tract | Medium over 30 years | Judgment against a household | POA owns and insures the shared assets and its members are not liable for its obligations (BOC § 22.152); shop rules and signed releases; recreational-use limits under CPRC ch. 75; see [[Entity and Ownership Design#Liability protection for the properties]] |
