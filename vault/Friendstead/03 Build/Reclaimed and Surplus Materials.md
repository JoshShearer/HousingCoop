# Reclaimed and Surplus Materials

Where used, surplus and seconds materials can cut the Friendstead budget, where the code, the lender and the group's own hours say no, and which Central Texas channels are worth working. Part of [[Friendstead]]; the budget lines it touches are in [[Per-Member Cost Model]] and [[Build Systems Compared]]; the hours rules it has to live inside are in [[Labor Rules and Fairness]].

> [!abstract] The short answer
> **Reclaim is worth $15,000 to $25,000 per household, 2 to 4% of the $650,000 all-in figure, and most of it is finishes.** Cabinets, counters, doors, fixtures, lighting and appliances from Austin teardowns and the ReStores are the reliable wins. Crushed-concrete road base, reclaimed roof-foam for the houses' continuous insulation and manufacturer seconds for the barndominium skin are the shared-pot wins if the engineer and the suppliers cooperate. Structural lumber, windows for the net-zero houses, wiring, used plumbing and anything from before 1980 with paint or mastic on it are not worth the trouble **[J]**.
>
> **Hours are the real constraint, not money.** Taking a whole house apart returns $15 to $40 of materials per member hour; the cost model values a member hour at about $80 on average and $125 or more on shell work. A two-day soft strip of the same house (kitchen, doors, fixtures, flooring) returns $125 to $235 an hour **[J]**. So the rule is: strip, never deconstruct, and never trade labor for materials, because materials received for work are barter income at fair market value (IRS Topic 420) **[S139]**.

Adding the house lines gives $10,000 to $20,000 a house. Adding the shared lines gives $20,000 to $45,000 across the project, $3,000 to $7,500 a household at six. Call it $15,000 to $25,000 per household, with the house finishes the dependable part and the shared items the ones that need an engineer's yes and a supplier's phone call.

## The teardown opportunity, and why to strip rather than deconstruct

Austin issues roughly 30 single-family total-demolition permits a month (33 in one 30-day window in mid-2026, a third-party count) **[S151]**. Demolition of a 1,500 to 2,000 sq ft house costs the owner about $13,000 to $35,000 in Austin at October 2026 prices **[S151]**. Deconstruction, taking the house apart for reuse, needs two to four times the people and 12 to 15 days for a 2,000 sq ft house against two days with an excavator; a Palo Alto pilot ran $35.84 per sq ft with a four-person crew over 14 days **[S137]**. Owners pay that premium for the charitable deduction, and the deduction is fragile: the Fourth Circuit in Mann and the Tax Court in Loube (2020) denied deductions of $300,000 or so because the donor had not given the entire interest in the house or the appraisal was not qualified **[S138]**. The ReUse People, the national deconstruction nonprofit, has an Austin representative who had done 29 deconstructions and 23 house relocations by 2019 **[S137]**.

What this means for Friendstead:

1. **Do not run deconstructions.** A 2,000 sq ft house is about 600 member hours (six people, 13 days) and yields perhaps $10,000 to $25,000 of material at ReStore replacement prices, $15 to $40 an hour **[J]**. Those hours are owed to the shared pot at $80 or more.
2. **Do run soft strips.** Two days, four people, 60 to 80 hours: kitchen, baths, doors and hardware, lighting, fans, hardwood flooring, water heater if new, appliances. That is most of the value for a tenth of the hours.
3. **Buy, never trade.** Materials received in return for stripping or clearing a site are barter income at fair market value for the person who received them **[S139]**. The value of used fixtures is low, but the group has decided not to run that risk anywhere. So a strip happens either on a listing that is already free for the taking (a gift, no service rendered) or under a one-page purchase of the salvage rights for a stated cash price, the owner's demolition contractor following behind. The same rule keeps the barter question out of the hours ledger ([[Labor Rules and Fairness]]).
4. **Let the ReStore do the rest.** Donating the strip's surplus to the ReStore is a deduction for whoever owns it, not a problem.

### What the ReStores will and will not take

Austin Habitat runs three ReStores: 500 W Ben White Blvd, 13804 N US-183 and 2521 S IH-35 in San Marcos, with free pickup on three to four days' notice **[S140]**. Their donation guidelines are a fair proxy for what sells and what a soft strip should bother with. Accepted: undamaged double-pane windows, unpainted doors in good condition, lumber over 3 ft with no paint, nails or screws, half sheets or better of plywood, complete cabinets, stone and solid-surface tops, sinks, new-in-box toilets at 1.28 gpf, full bundles of shingles, corrugated roofing and roofing tin, insulation in new packaging, working appliances. Refused: single-pane windows, painted doors, 8 ft doors, used toilets and tubs, laminate and cultured-marble tops, used flooring of any kind (engineered hardwood by exception), used asphalt roofing, used insulation, anything with asbestos **[S140]**. Chapters generally price new stock at about half of retail and used stock lower; one chapter's list runs windows $25 to $1,200, cabinets $10 to $150 and doors $5 to $1,000, with periodic half-price sales **[S141]**.

Austin's Construction and Demolition Recycling Ordinance helps indirectly. Projects over 5,000 sq ft and all commercial and multifamily demolitions must reuse or recycle 50% of their debris, and the City points contractors at reuse organisations and lists its processors **[S136]**. It does not require deconstruction or salvage of houses, so the single-family teardown stream is unorganised and is reached by the contractors and the permit feed, not by a programme.

## Code and lender gates

The group's homesites are outside any city. The county is not the gate; the group's own inspector, engineer, lender and insurer are.

- **Who inspects.** Where a county has adopted Subchapter F of Chapter 233 (Bastrop did, by resolution on 14 September 2009), new houses must meet the 2008 IRC or the county seat's version, and the builder must hire a licensed engineer, architect, TREC inspector or ICC residential combination inspector for three inspections: foundation before the pour, framing and mechanicals before cover, and completion ([Tex. Loc. Gov't Code §§ 233.152 to 233.154](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/LG/htm/LG.233.htm#233.153)). Bastrop County issues no permits, does no inspections and asks for no reports **[S142]**. So any reused material has to satisfy the inspector the group hires, and that inspector signs the report the construction lender and the insurer will ask for. [[Jurisdiction and Feasibility]] should carry this subchapter; it does not yet.
- **Used materials in the IRC.** Used materials may be reused where they meet the code's requirements for new materials; used equipment and devices only with the building official's approval (IRC R104.9.1, "Materials and equipment reuse" in the 2024 edition) **[S144]**. Load-bearing lumber, joists and rafters must carry a grade mark or a certificate of inspection from an accredited grading agency (R502.1, R602.1, R802.1). No grading rule exists specifically for reclaimed lumber; the Forest Products Laboratory's work on one never became a standard **[S144]**. In practice: reclaimed lumber is blocking, bracing, forms, partitions, shop fittings and furniture. Not studs, not joists, not headers.
- **Energy.** The statewide residential energy code is the 2015 IRC energy chapter (Health & Safety Code § 388.003); outside a municipality compliance is shown by an accredited programme, a private code-certified inspector or the builder's own certification form, kept three years (§ 388.004) **[S143]**. The net-zero package in [[Zero Energy Passive Design]] is well past code, so the issue is not passing the code but keeping the modelled performance: an unlabelled used window is a hole in the energy model.
- **Lender and insurer.** A construction lender's draw inspector values work in place at cost and an appraiser values the finished house against comparables; neither credits salvage above what it would have cost new, and builder's-risk underwriters commonly exclude used electrical and mechanical equipment **[J]**. Keep receipts for salvage purchases: they are the basis the lender will see.

## Health and safety

- **Asbestos.** Houses before the mid-1980s commonly carry asbestos in 9 in floor tile and its mastic, joint compound, textured ceilings, cement siding and duct wrap. Demolition of one private residence is exempt from the asbestos NESHAP notification that applies to other buildings (DSHS decision tree; 25 Tex. Admin. Code § 296.251), and asbestos waste still has to go to a TCEQ-permitted landfill by a licensed transporter **[S150]**. Exemption from notice is not exemption from the hazard: no strip touches floor tile, popcorn ceilings or old siding, and a $400 to $600 survey is cheap insurance on anything older than 1985 **[J]**.
- **Lead.** The EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting rule does not bind a homeowner working on their own house, but the practices do, and pre-1978 paint is the reason the ReStore refuses painted lumber and doors **[S150]**. Rule for the group: painted wood from before 1978 is not taken.
- **Off-site injury.** The tract's builder's-risk and general-liability policies do not follow a crew to a stranger's house. The broker brief in [[Friendstead Risks]] should add off-premises work, and every strip runs under a written release with the property owner and the three-strike safety rule in [[Labor Rules and Fairness]].

## Hours, money and storage

- **Whose hours.** A strip scheduled by the managers for shared-asset material (barndominium doors and partitions, fence pipe, shop fittings) is a scheduled task and counts as crew hours even though it is off-site, which the 20% off-site cap in [[Labor Rules and Fairness]] should say in terms. A strip for your own kitchen is your own time and your own truck, exactly like your own house's framing.
- **Whose money.** Salvage for shared assets is bought by the LLC and sits in the shared pot like any other material. Salvage for a house is bought by that household on its own draw, consistent with the materials rule in the labor note. No household ever "contributes" salvage to the pot in lieu of cash; that is a contribution of property with a valuation argument attached.
- **Storage before the barndominium.** Nothing can be accumulated until it can be kept dry and off the ground; Central Texas humidity and termites ruin stacked lumber in a season. The barndominium shop is the first dry storage the project has, which is one more reason to build it first. Before that, a used 40 ft container on the tract ($3,000 to $5,000 **[J]**) or nothing.
- **Design freeze.** Openings, cabinet runs and fixture locations stay soft until the salvage is in hand; the net-zero envelope, the structure and the mechanicals do not. A shared inventory sheet, posted weekly with the hours ledger, is how six households avoid buying the same kitchen twice.

## For the term sheet and the labor exhibit

- Reuse policy: finishes and fittings yes, structure and envelope performance no; the inspector the group hires is the authority on anything in between.
- No labor-for-materials exchanges by the LLC or by members acting for it; salvage rights are bought for cash in writing or taken from an unconditional free listing.
- Managers may schedule off-site salvage days for shared assets; they count as crew hours and are exempt from the off-site cap.
- Pre-1985 strips need an asbestos screen; pre-1978 painted wood is not taken; off-premises cover and a property-owner release before the first strip.
- One salvage inventory, posted weekly; one reclaimed-foam order and one seconds order for all six houses, placed by the managers.
