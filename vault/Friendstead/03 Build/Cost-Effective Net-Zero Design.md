# Cost-Effective Net-Zero Design

The cheapest net-zero 2,000 sq ft house near Austin is a plain single-story rectangle, stick-framed in 2x6 at 24 in on center by members, with the money spent on air sealing, ducts inside the envelope, a deep vented attic, low-gain windows and right-sized all-electric equipment, and the rest bought with panels. At October 2026 prices a rooftop kilowatt-hour costs about $1.50 of first cost per annual kWh produced **[S103]**; the water heater, right-sized heat pump and ducts inside save one for $1 to $2, low-gain windows, air sealing and 2x6 walls for $3 to $5, and exterior foam, double-stud, ICF, SIPs and the Phius package for $8 to $40 **[M]**. The envelope should stop where it meets the panel price, which is roughly the DOE Zero Energy Ready Home line. Full analysis: [Zero Energy Passive Design Feasibility, tab "Cost-effective design"](https://claude.ai/artifact/6EXUtrMb4YmZxhAid7Ex2b). Part of [[Friendstead]]; the barndominium case in [[Zero Energy Passive Design]]; baseline costs in [[Build Systems Compared]] and [[Energy Costs by Envelope]].

## Form

32 x 62 ft rectangle, ridge east-west, 4:12 gable on raised-heel trusses at 24 in, 9 ft flat ceilings, slab on grade, vented R-49 attic, ducts in a dropped hall ceiling, 12 to 15% window-to-wall mostly south under an 8 to 10 ft porch, almost no west glass, wet core on one wall. Two-story saves $10 to $20 per sq ft at contractor prices **[S125]** but less on a self-build and leaves only about 550 sq ft of south roof; decide per household. No basement, crawl space, vault or interior thermal mass.

## Systems compared

Same 2,000 sq ft house, co-op path, members doing all doable work; first cost from [[Build Systems Compared]] plus the ZERH package below; 20-year total adds a net-zero array at $2.17 per W and 1,450 kWh per kW and the residual co-op bill (fixed charges plus the 15 cent retail to 7 cent export gap on exported production), 3% escalation. All **[M]**.

The recommended house is $15,000 over the code house up front and about $5,000 over it across 20 years: a wash on money, a clear win on comfort, humidity control, equipment size and freeze resilience. Every system above it pays more for the shell than the array it displaces. The post-frame rows are cheaper because the shell is cheaper, not because the envelope is better; a post-frame shell with lap siding, a conventional roofline and porches is the version to ask a lender about. PNNL's Texas code study anchors the cheap end: 2015 to 2021 IECC in zone 2A costs $1,280 on a slab house, saves $173 a year, pays back in 7.0 years **[S121]**.

## The package (about $15,000 over code, before the array)

Monolithic slab, no slab insulation, sill gasketed; 2x6 at 24 in advanced framing, raised heels ($1,500 net) **[S122]**; taped sheathing air barrier ($800); R-20 blown cavity ($0 to $600); vented R-49 attic ($1,200); U 0.30 / SHGC 0.22 windows ($1,000); ducts in a dropped hall ceiling ($1,500) **[S123]**; two blower-door tests to 2.5 ACH50 ($900), AeroBarrier only if rough-in fails; 2-ton inverter heat pump by Manual J and S ($1,000 net over a 3.5-ton split) **[S124]**; ventilating dehumidifier at 50% RH with the 62.2 outdoor-air intake ($3,000), no ERV **[S124]**; heat-pump water heater ($1,200 over a tank) **[S124]**; LED and ENERGY STAR appliances, induction ($500); light standing-seam metal roof if member-installed ($4,000 to $6,000 over shingles) **[S126]**; solar-ready conduit and panel ($400); HERS rating with the ZERH checklist ($1,500 to $3,000). Array 6.5 kW, 16 to 17 panels on seam clamps, $14,000 installed or about $11,000 member-racked **[S103]** **[S127]**. Left out on purpose: spray foam, exterior continuous insulation, ERV, slab insulation, triple glazing, vaulted ceilings.

## What it changes

The barndominium keeps its Phase 1 and shop role and its tab 1 spec but is no longer the template for the member homes. Member homes are stick 2x6 unless two lenders and an insurer accept post-frame, which is $75,000 cheaper. One plan built six times fits the [[Further Cost Levers]] bulk-plan lever. [[Per-Member Cost Model]] gets three opt-in lines per house: ZERH package about $15,000, array $11,000 to $14,000, battery $12,000 to $16,000; the home segment moves from $290,000 to about $305,000 before solar. [[Energy Costs by Envelope]] gets the recommended house at 9,500 kWh. [[Member Labor Matrix]] gains taping, blowing insulation, framing the duct chase and racking panels, and loses wall layout if the exterior walls come as plant panels ([[Prefab Wall Panels]]); commissioning stays with the HVAC contractor. A HERS rater and a mechanical designer join the professional list at $3,000 to $6,000 a house.
