# Building Materials Price History

Materials are not expensive because of lumber. Lumber is the only major input that fell back after 2021; everything else rose 40 to 110% from January 2020 and stayed there, and the composite index for residential construction goods is up 56% **[S43]**. The material figures in [[Build Systems Compared]] come from 2024 to 2026 sources, so they already carry that level. On timber frames specifically, a Texas fabricator prices a full frame package at $50,000 to $120,000, which is a frame only; the $140,000 to $180,000 kit in the comparison includes the SIP enclosure, so the two numbers are not the same product. Part of [[Friendstead]]; sources in [[Friendstead Sources]].

All series are the Bureau of Labor Statistics producer price index (PPI), monthly and not seasonally adjusted, through September 2026, pulled from FRED on 22 Oct 2026 and indexed to January 2020 = 100 so they share one axis. PPI measures what mills and plants charge, not retail; a lumber-yard price moves with it but adds a margin.

## Wood

![[friendstead-ppi-wood.png]]

Softwood lumber peaked in May 2021 at 2.6 times its January 2020 price, fell by half, and has drifted back up over the last year, partly on Canadian softwood duties. It is still about 30% above January 2020. Plywood and OSB held more of the spike: structural panels sit 67% above January 2020.

## Concrete, aggregates and drywall

![[friendstead-ppi-concrete.png]]

None of these came down. Ready-mix is up 40% since January 2020 and at its record; sand, gravel and crushed stone are up 57%, and the whole group has climbed 1 to 6% in the last twelve months. A slab, driveway, road base and septic rock are the parts of the project with no price relief in sight **[J]**.

## Metals and the envelope

![[friendstead-ppi-metals.png]]

Steel mill products doubled in 2021, fell, and have doubled again since early 2025 on tariffs: up 28% in twelve months and 107% since January 2020. Pre-engineered metal buildings, the barndominium's shell, track steel with a lag and sit 72% above January 2020. Copper and aluminum, which drive wiring and HVAC cost, are at a record. Insulation is up 52%.

## The composite view

![[friendstead-ppi-overview.png]]

Construction materials as a group rose about 2% a year for two decades, then 55 to 60% in two years, and have started climbing again in 2026: the all-materials index is up 11% in twelve months.

## What this does to the Friendstead numbers

- **Stick-built** carries the most lumber and panels, the one group with a real chance of falling further; it is also the system most exposed to the 2026 lumber upturn. The $229,000 material figure in [[Build Systems Compared]] is a 2026-level number, not a 2019 one **[M]**.
- **Barndominium** material is steel, and steel is up 28% in a year. A shell quoted in 2025 is stale; get a fresh quote with a short validity window before relying on the $80,000 shell figure **[J]**.
- **Every system** shares the slab, septic, road base, wiring and HVAC, where concrete, aggregates and copper are at records. Those lines are the same across the three systems and are the reason the project cannot escape the post-2020 level by choosing a cheaper frame.
- **Timing.** Lumber is seasonal and cyclical; concrete and steel have been one-way. Buying lumber in a winter trough and locking a steel quote early is worth more than waiting for a general decline that the composite index does not show **[J]**.

## The timber frame question

Nautilus Timber Frames in Taylor, Texas publishes these ranges on its pricing page **[S44]**: trusses $3,000 to $15,000 each; porch and entry frames $8,000 to $60,000; interior beam packages $8,000 to $30,000; pavilion and carport frames $25,000 to $80,000; full timber packages $50,000 to $120,000 and up, with most residential packages between $25,000 and $80,000. Packages include cut and labeled timbers, joinery, hardware and pegs, installation drawings and delivery in Texas; engineering is "when required" and no price per square foot is given. The Hill Country package the group looked at is $88,000 in Douglas fir ($60,000 frame plus $28,000 material package) and covers porch framing, interior ceiling beams, a carport and rafter tails for a custom residence, not a full structural frame **[S45]**. The Gulf Coast package is $40,000 for five curved trusses with purlins and ridge **[S46]**.

The comparison note's timber row used $140,000 to $180,000 for the kit and $600,000 contractor all-in, built from Hamill Creek's $60 to $90 per square foot kit range and Trinity's $269 per square foot package price **[S17]** **[S18]**. Those figures are for a complete frame-plus-SIP enclosure delivered as one package. Nautilus sells the frame alone. Put side by side for 2,000 sq ft **[M]**:

| Package | Frame | Enclosure | Approximate cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nautilus full timber package | $80,000 to $120,000 for a frame that carries the whole roof **[S44]** | Add SIP walls and roof panels, roughly $40,000 to $60,000 delivered **[J]**, plus crane and panel crew | $130,000 to $190,000, in line with the comparison note |
| Nautilus Hill Country style hybrid | $60,000 to $90,000 of porch, ceiling and carport timbers on a stick-framed house **[S45]** | Conventional 2x4 framing members do the structural work | Stick-built co-op cost $290,000 plus about $80,000 timber and $10,000 of crane and lead-carpenter time, less about $10,000 of displaced framing: **about $370,000**, against $433,000 for the full timber and SIP system |

The hybrid is the cheaper way to get the timber look, and members can do most of the stick framing around it. It gives up the SIP energy and speed benefits in [[Energy Costs by Envelope]] unless SIPs are used for the shell anyway. A quote from Nautilus on a specific plan is the next step; their lead time is 6 to 10 weeks from approved drawings.
