# Prefab Wall Panels

Factory wall panels do not save the co-op money; they buy back member hours. On the co-op path the framing labor is already free, so open panels from a truss plant add about $3,000 to $5,000 of cash per 2,000 sq ft house (panel premium, delivery, a day or two of lift) and give back roughly 150 to 220 member hours, about 60% of the wall-framing phase, at $15 to $30 per hour bought **[M]**. That is cheaper than hiring framers at bid rates and far below the $125 to $135 a shell hour is worth in [[Build Systems Compared]]. Do it for the exterior walls; price the interior partitions as an option and take them only if the premium is small and the plan is frozen. Full analysis: [Zero Energy Passive Design Feasibility, tab "Prefab walls"](https://claude.ai/artifact/6EXUtrMb4YmZxhAid7Ex2b). Part of [[Friendstead]]; the house it applies to is in [[Cost-Effective Net-Zero Design]]; who frames in [[Member Labor Matrix]]; the structural-panel case (SIPs) is separate, in [[Self-Made SIP Panels]].

## What the evidence says

Every controlled comparison finds the same shape: panels cut framing hours by half or more and waste by most, while the material package costs the same or a little more, so a builder paying framers by the square foot sees no saving unless the framer reprices.

| Study | What was compared | Result |
|---|---|---|
| SBCA Framing the American Dream, 2015 **[S128]** | Two 2,900 sq ft houses side by side, stick against full components (floor trusses, wall panels, roof trusses) | 152.1 against 373.5 crew hours to erect; 11,758 against 20,643 board feet; waste three trash cans against a 15-yard dumpster. The 1995 round put the walls alone at 26.5 against 66.5 hours (flyer, search excerpt) |
| National Housing Endowment / University of Denver, 2019–2021 **[S129]** | Paired houses with two national builders, including identical 1,996 sq ft single-story slab ranches in Texas | Framing cycle 2 to 6 days shorter, two fewer framers per crew, about $1 per sq ft of framing labor and $2,500 per house, one to three fewer dumpster hauls; builders miss the saving because framers price per square foot |
| BMC Ready-Frame precut study, Denver **[S130]** | 12 houses averaging 2,397 sq ft, six precut and six stick | 20% fewer days with less experienced labor and no lead carpenter; one dumpster haul against three; 1,947 fewer board feet; supplier-commissioned |
| UBS production-builder analysis, June 2025 **[S131]** | Five production builders, open wall panels against stick | Per house: labor +$4,921, material −$3,166, fewer variance orders +$1,906, cycle time +$2,024, crane and equipment −$1,400; $6,175 of value across the chain but the builder alone is $783 worse off, so adoption stays near 8 to 10% |
| Trade and forum accounts **[S132]** | Builders and framers on panel pricing and fit | Panel package 10% to 40% over loose lumber; "at least as much, usually slightly more"; exterior walls set in two hours; complaints about panels not straight, wrong-side sheathing and foundations out of square; no crane needed on a single story if the crew moves the panels |

## The model for the 32 x 62 ft house

Same house as [[Cost-Effective Net-Zero Design]]: 188 ft of 9 ft 2x6 exterior wall (about 1,700 sq ft) and about 250 ft of 2x4 partition (about 2,250 sq ft). Loose lumber and sheathing for all the walls is about $5,000 at October 2026 prices (SPF studs $405 and 2x4 $454 per thousand board feet at the mill, roughly 1.8 times that at the yard) **[S118]** **[M]**.

| **Extra cash per house** | **$2,000 to $4,000** | **$700 to $1,400** | **$300 to $800** |
| Member hours saved | 90 to 130 of about 130 to 180 | 60 to 90 of about 120 to 170 | 40 to 80 across both |

| What it costs you | Plan frozen 4 to 8 weeks ahead; slab square and level within about 1/4 in **[S134]**; truck access for a 48 ft flatbed; panels tarped until set | Flexibility to move a partition on framing day; the easiest practice walls for new framers | Little; the Ready-Frame plant nearest Austin is in Carrollton (DFW), so ask the local Builders FirstSource yard whether it ships here |

All **[M]**. Amateur hours use the vault's 1.3 to 1.5 slowdown on the crew hours above, weighted to layout and cutting, which is where new framers lose most time. Over six houses the exterior panels are $12,000 to $24,000 of cash for 550 to 800 member hours, about half a household's 1,500-hour pledge.

## Fit with the net-zero spec

Panels are stick framing built flat in a plant, so lenders, appraisers and insurers see a normal house, unlike post-frame. The plant's layout drawings satisfy the 2015 IRC baseline in Tex. Health & Safety Code § 388.003 and give the chapter 233 inspector, where there is one, something to check. Three things to write into the order:

- **Advanced framing is not the plant default.** Specify 2x6 at 24 in, single top plate with trusses stacked on studs, two-stud corners, ladder blocking at partitions and right-sized headers; most plants quote double top plates and three-stud corners unless told otherwise.
- **The air barrier.** Factory sheathing adds a joint at every panel end on top of the usual 4 ft joints; all of them get taped on site as the taped-sheathing air barrier in [[Cost-Effective Net-Zero Design]] already assumes. Ask whether the plant sheathes with Zip or commodity OSB, and price both.
- **Trusses and panels from one plant.** Lockhart Truss in Red Rock (Bastrop County) and Allied Truss Texas (Austin service area) make both **[S132]**; one vendor keeps the raised-heel truss bearing and the wall layout on the same drawing and leaves one party responsible for fit.

## What it changes

[[Member Labor Matrix]]: the framing row gains "exterior walls as plant panels; members set, plumb, brace and tape; the carpenter-mentor is needed for the set day, not for layout." [[Build Systems Compared]]: the stick-built row's 3,000 amateur hours fall to about 2,800 with exterior panels, for $2,000 to $4,000 more cash; the post-frame comparison does not move, because post-frame has no stud walls to panelize beyond its infill. [[Per-Member Cost Model]]: an opt-in line of $3,000 to $5,000 per house. One plan built six times is exactly the repetition a panel plant prices well, which strengthens the one-plan lever in [[Further Cost Levers]]. Decision gate: when the plan is frozen, get a loose-lumber takeoff, an open-panel quote (exterior only, and exterior plus interior) and a truss quote from the same plant, plus a precut quote from a Builders FirstSource yard; take exterior panels if the premium is under about 30% of the takeoff.
