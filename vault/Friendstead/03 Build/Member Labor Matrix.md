# Member Labor Matrix

Texas has no state license for general contractors or carpenters, and the owner-occupant exemptions for electrical, plumbing and HVAC cover a person working on their own home. Those exemptions do not cover a friend working on your house, so under the co-op the licensed trades on each house are either done by that house's owner alone or hired. Part of [[Friendstead]]; cost effect in [[Build Systems Compared]].

| Task | Share of a stick-built house **[S6]** | Members | Must hire | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Survey, plat, civil engineering, drainage | 2 to 4% of project | No | RPLS surveyor, P.E. | Sealed drawings required for the plat and the county |
| Clearing, grading, trenching | Site work 7.6% | Operate a rented skid steer or mini-excavator after training | Dozer and excavator with operator for the road and pads | Equipment, not skill |
| Road base and paving | Shared | Gravel spreading | Paving crew if the county requires pavement | Equipment and county road standards |
| Well | Lot utility | No | Licensed water well driller and pump installer | State license; groundwater district permit |
| Septic | Lot utility | A homeowner may install their own conventional system after a licensed site evaluation; aerobic units usually need a Class II installer | Site evaluator, installer for aerobic, two-year maintenance contract | 30 TAC 285 and the county order |
| Foundation | 10.5% | Forms, rebar, vapor barrier, finishing help | Concrete pump and finishing crew; engineer's slab design | Timing and skill; a bad slab is unfixable |
| Framing | 16.6% | Yes, with one experienced lead; a pre-cut steel kit removes cutting and layout but needs screw guns, snips and a lift for trusses | Crane and panel or frame crew for SIP and timber frame | Members' largest saving on stick-built and the steel kit |
| Roofing | In exterior 13.4% | Metal roof, yes | Steep or complex roofs | Fall protection and insurance |
| Windows, doors, siding, exterior trim | In exterior 13.4% | Yes | None | Standard member work |
| Electrical | In rough-ins 19.2% | Only the owner on their own home, Occupations Code 1305.003 exemption **[S31]**; utility sets the meter | Licensed electrician for everyone else's house and the service entrance | License; lender and insurer may require licensed sign-off |
| Plumbing | In rough-ins | Only the owner on their own homestead (Occupations Code 1301.051, not opened; verify) | Licensed plumber otherwise; gas lines always | License; pressure test for inspection |
| HVAC | In rough-ins | Owner on their own home under 16 TAC 75.30, but refrigerant handling needs EPA 608 | Licensed HVAC contractor in practice | Equipment warranty and refrigerant rules |
| Insulation | In rough-ins | Batts and blown-in, yes | Spray foam crew | Equipment and chemicals |
| Drywall, tape, texture | In interior 24.1% | Yes; texture takes practice | None | Largest interior labor block |
| Paint, flooring, tile, cabinets, trim, fixtures | In interior 24.1% | Yes | Countertop fabrication | Standard member work |
| Inspections | Final 6.5% | No | Licensed inspector, engineer or ICC-certified inspector for the three chapter 233 inspections where the county has opted in **[S32]**; lender's inspector for draws | Statute |
| Project management, procurement, bookkeeping, meals, childcare on work days | Overhead | Yes, and counts toward the hour target | None | Keeps crews working |

Two practical rules. Every house needs one experienced lead per trade phase; budget a paid site superintendent or carpenter-mentor at $60 to $90 an hour for the first framing and first drywall job, and the learning transfers to the next five houses **[J]**. And the insurer, not the statute, decides what members may do: get the occupational-accident and builder's-risk program in writing before the labor matrix is final.
