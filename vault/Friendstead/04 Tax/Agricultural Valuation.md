# Agricultural Valuation

Buy a tract that already carries 1-d-1 open-space valuation, keep the use running through construction, and put bees on the 10-acre common tract: worth roughly $5,000 a year to the group with no waiting period **[M]**. Budget a rollback of about $5,000 per homesite when the lots convert to residential use. Part of [[Friendstead]]; see [[Tax and Code Savings]].

## How it works

- **Qualifying.** Land must be devoted principally to agriculture to the degree of intensity typical for the county, and must have been for five of the preceding seven years, Tax Code § 23.51 **[S25]**. A tract bought with the valuation in place carries its history; a tract without it waits five years.
- **Beekeeping** is agriculture by statute on not less than 5 and not more than 20 acres **[S25]**. Every county opened requires six colonies on the first five acres; Bastrop and Caldwell add one hive per 2.5 acres **[S29]** **[S30]**. Hays and the Travis extension office exclude the homesite, so a 5-acre homestead lot cannot qualify by itself **[S28]** **[S27]**.
- **Wildlife management** is only a conversion from land already under ag or timber appraisal, with at least three of seven practices and a county minimum tract size **[S26]**. Hays requires 14.29 acres standalone or 12.5 acres per member of a wildlife property association **[S28]**, so a 10-acre common tract fails there.
- **Homesite.** The appraisal district carves out the residence and the land around it at market value; the homestead exemption can cover up to 20 acres, and in Hays any acreage under a homestead exemption is out of the ag calculation **[S28]**.
- **Rollback.** When qualified land changes use, the owner owes the difference between the ag tax and the market-value tax for the three years preceding the change (Tax Code § 23.55(a), cut from five years by H.B. 1743 in 2019; the interest charge was removed by H.B. 3833 in 2021). If only part of a parcel changes use, only that part is charged (§ 23.55(d)). The chief appraiser determines the change and the owner may protest it (§ 23.55(e)) **[S85]**. The Hays and Caldwell packets opened still print the old five-year text; the statute controls. A new owner must file a new application before 1 May or lose the valuation for that year (§ 23.54(d), (e)) **[S85]**.

## What it is worth on this tract

| Scenario | Annual effect | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 10-acre common tract under beekeeping, east side | About $5,500 saved for the group, about $900 per household **[M]** | Market value $280,000 at about 2% total rate versus a productivity value of a few hundred dollars an acre; 9 hives cost about $4,000 to set up **[J]** |
| Same tract under grazing | Fails the 15 to 25 acre minimums in Bastrop, Hays and Travis; Caldwell allows a contiguous neighbor's operation to count | Lease to a neighbor only where the county allows combining |
| A 6-acre or larger homesite with its own bees | About $2,000 to $2,800 saved on the acreage beyond the homesite **[M]** | Tier C lots at 6 acres instead of 5 make this possible; 2 and 3 acre lots cannot qualify |
| Rollback on conversion of a 3-acre lot | About $5,000 once, plus interest **[M]** | Three years of the tax difference on $84,000 of market value; budget $5,000 per lot |
| Wildlife management on the common tract | Not available at 10 acres in Hays; unknown in Bastrop and Caldwell | Only worth pursuing on a 15-acre or larger common tract |

The common tract should be sized with this in mind: 10 acres is enough for bees in every county; 15 acres opens grazing in Bastrop and wildlife in Hays with an association, at a land cost of another $140,000 east or $300,000 in the Hill Country **[M]**.
