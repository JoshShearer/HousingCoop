# Energy Incentives

What federal, Texas, utility and USDA programs would actually pay a Friendstead household or the association for building efficient homes, solar, storage, rainwater and electrified mechanicals, as of 24 Oct 2026, for a build that closes on land in 2027 and moves in during 2028. Short version: the federal credits that would have been worth about $15,000 a home are gone, and what is left is a property-tax exemption on solar, a sales-tax exemption on rainwater equipment, a state rebate program that has not launched and is income-limited, and the co-op utility's interconnection terms. Part of [[Friendstead]]; levers in [[Tax and Code Savings]]; envelope and bills in [[Energy Costs by Envelope]]. Nothing here is tax advice.

## What ended, and what it would have been worth

Public Law 119-21 (4 July 2025) cut off the residential and builder credits that an owner-built home finishing in 2028 would otherwise have claimed **[S87]**.

| Credit | What it paid | Cut-off | For this build |
|---|---|---|---|
| IRC § 25D, residential clean energy | 30% of solar, battery, geothermal heat pump cost, no cap | Expenditures after 31 Dec 2025 | Gone. About $7,500 on a $25,000 array and battery |
| IRC § 25C, energy-efficient home improvement | 30% up to $2,000 for a heat pump or heat-pump water heater, $1,200 for insulation, windows, doors, panel | Property placed in service after 31 Dec 2025 | Gone; it applied to existing homes anyway |
| IRC § 45L, new energy-efficient home credit | $2,500 to the builder for an ENERGY STAR home, $5,000 for a DOE Zero Energy Ready Home; the "eligible contractor" is the person who constructed the home, so an owner-builder could claim it | Homes acquired after 30 June 2026 | Gone |
| IRC § 30C, EV charger | 30% up to $1,000, in rural or low-income census tracts only | Placed in service after 30 June 2026 | Gone |
| IRC § 48E, third-party-owned solar (lease or PPA) | 30% to the lessor | No credit for leased residential solar (§ 48E(i)); solar facilities placed in service after 31 Dec 2027 | Gone either way |
| IRC § 179D, commercial buildings | Deduction per sq ft | Construction beginning after 30 June 2026 | Never applied |

Together about $14,000 to $16,000 a home at 2025 rules, and $5,000 of it was tied to certifying the house, which the group should still consider for the reasons below.

## What the covenants must allow

Texas already forbids an owners' association from banning the things this project wants. Prop. Code § 202.007 voids any covenant that restricts composting, rain barrels and rainwater systems, drip irrigation, or drought-resistant landscaping; § 202.010 voids any covenant that prohibits a solar energy device, subject to listed limits (common property, the development period, placement and colour rules that do not cut output more than 10%) **[S90]**. The declaration should say so affirmatively, and should carve the association's own solar on the barndominium out of the common-property exception by resolution, since § 202.010(d)(2) and (3) let an association restrict devices on common property. Add to [[Term Sheet Decisions]].

## What to do with this

- **Still certify the houses.** A HERS rating costs about $1,500 to $3,000 a home **[J]** and now buys an energy-efficient mortgage, an Oncor payment if that is the utility, and a resale document, but no federal credit. Decide per house, not per group.
- **File the two Texas exemptions as routine.** Form 50-123 on every array including the association's, and the sales-tax certificate on every rainwater purchase; the savings are small but certain.
- **Size solar for self-consumption on Bluebonnet and PEC.** Both credit exports at about 6.7 to 7.2 cents and expire unused credit yearly; the design consequences are in [[Zero Energy Passive Design]]. With wholesale-rate exports and no 30% credit, payback on an oversized array is poor; batteries carry the § 11.27 exemption too.
- **Keep a watch item for Texas Home Energy Rebates.** If the programme opens before the mechanicals are bought and allows new construction, income-qualified households could get heat pumps and water heaters at half price.
- **Do not count on federal money returning.** The 2025 law set the cut-offs in the statute; a later Congress would have to re-enact them.
