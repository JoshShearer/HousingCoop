# Smaller Homesite Arrangement

Homesites of 0.5 to 2 acres on an 18 to 20 acre tract bring an east-side household to about **$580,000 to $630,000** all-in, $65,000 to $70,000 below the $650,000 to $695,000 of the 2 to 5 acre arrangement in [[Per-Member Cost Model]] **[M]**. The 10-acre common tract stays. This is an alternative under study, not a replacement: the existing notes are unchanged. Part of [[Friendstead]]; full write-up in the Claude Doc [Friendstead: 0.5 to 2 acre parcel arrangement](https://claude.ai/artifact/Tt2AsaWwtQWzigcnn2LQts).

## Rules that bind

State septic rules allow half an acre only on a public water supply and require 1 acre on a private well (30 Tex. Admin. Code § 285.4(a)(1)) **[S154]**. A shared private well is not a public supply until it reaches 15 connections or 25 people **[S15]**.

| County | Public water + septic | Well + septic | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caldwell | 1 acre rural; minimum waived in a Conservation Subdivision (40% open space), leaving the state half acre | 1 acre | Both layouts; private gravel road on an association lot allowed **[S155]** |
| Bastrop | Half acre in a Suburban Subdivision (community water system) | 1 acre, average lot over 2 acres | Public water yes; wells only if the common tract counts in the average **[S8]** |

## Open questions

- Does Bastrop count a platted common tract in the 2-acre average for a Rural Subdivision?
- Will the water supplier serve six taps, and at what extension cost? The $6,000 tap is modeled from a 2021 Aqua filing seen only in a search excerpt.
- Deed restrictions on small tracts: check Schedule B before paying for engineering.
- Can an aerobic septic system and its spray area fit a half-acre clay lot?
- Does Bastrop accept a private association road where each lot must abut a public street?

Both arrangements are presets in the [member finance calculator](https://claude.ai/artifact/9JMzkMfRNipFKTboQC2Tbc), and the community app's cost page switches between them ([[Community App]]).
