# Further Cost Levers

Savings not in the base case of [[Per-Member Cost Model]]. The top five together are worth another $50,000 to $100,000 per household on top of sweat equity, which would bring the east-side co-op house under $600,000 **[M]**. Three of them (the seventh lot, seller financing, tract choice) must be decided before contracting, so they belong in [[Term Sheet Decisions]]. Part of [[Friendstead]]; sources in [[Friendstead Sources]].

## Large levers

| Lever | Worth per household | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| Plat a seventh lot and sell it | $10,000 to $18,000 **[M]** | A 5-acre lot bought at $28,000 an acre inside a 30-acre tract resells at $40,000 to $50,000 an acre **[S2]**. The gain pays most of the road. Costs one stranger neighbor (ROFR and covenants apply); the CPA must keep the LLC from being taxed as a dealer. |
| Seller financing on the land | $5,000 to $15,000 in fees and interest, plus cash kept for construction **[M]** | Tracts sit 220 to 250 days **[S2]**. Owner-carry at 10 to 20% down is common on rural Texas land and skips the 35% down, appraisal and origination of a land loan **[S42]**. Needs a seller who does not need cash. |
| Buy a tract that already has improvements | $5,000 to $45,000 **[M]** | An existing well, electric service, ag valuation, road frontage or a livable house. A usable house replaces the $275,000 barndominium outright. |
| One plan built six times, bought in bulk | $15,000 to $30,000 **[J]** | Stock plan plus a local engineer's stamp instead of custom design. Windows, roofing, lumber and fixtures ordered for six houses at pro-desk pricing. Licensed trades bid six houses at once. Crews get faster on each house. |
| Simpler shape and advanced framing | $15,000 to $30,000 **[J]** | Two-story rectangle, simple gable, 24-inch stud spacing, single top plate, two-stud corners, carport not garage. Less slab and roof per square foot and 5 to 10% less framing lumber. |
| Share wells in groups of three | $7,000 to $10,000 **[M]** | Two wells instead of six. Three households stay under the 15-connection, 25-person public water system line **[S15]**. Needs a recorded well-sharing agreement and a pressure tank per house. |
| Shorter road | up to $12,000 **[M]** | Every 1,000 feet of paved road is $75,000 shared. Lots fronting an existing county road, or flag lots with shared driveways, shrink it. Depends on the tract and on the county's layout rules **[S8]**. |
| Deed lots by distribution, not sale | $3,000 to $10,000 plus a tax bill avoided **[J]** | The LLC distributes lots to members rather than selling them; under federal partnership rules a distribution of property is generally not a taxable event, a sale is. Texas has no transfer tax. Distribution also keeps the household's pre-deed right out of the contract-for-deed rules in Prop. Code §§ 5.061–5.085 **[S69]**. A CPA item before the operating agreement is drafted. See [[Entity and Ownership Design]] and [[Friendstead Risks]]. |
| Own the equipment, then sell it | $5,000 to $10,000 **[M]** | A used skid steer and mini-excavator cost $40,000 to $60,000 and resell near 70%; renting the same machines for 24 months runs $60,000 to $80,000. Someone maintains them. |
| Conventional septic instead of aerobic | $10,000 **[S5]** | Only where the soil allows. Site evaluations during feasibility show which lots qualify and should drive lot assignment. |

## Smaller or situational

- **Texas Veterans Land Board loan.** A veteran member can borrow up to $200,000 at 7.25% over 30 years with 5% down, limits raised 9 Feb 2026 **[S55]**. Only on a lot in the veteran's own name, so after plat; cheaper than any bank lot loan.
- **Electric line extension allowances.** Pedernales and Bluebonnet give free footage per new meter **[J]**, unverified. Clustering homes near the existing line and running one backbone can cut the $60,000 electric budget. Verify with the co-op serving the tract.
- **Owner-maintained aerobic septic.** A 2007 law let homeowners maintain their own aerobic units after training **[S56]**, but counties can still require a contract and some do. Saves $300 to $600 a year per house where allowed; check the target county's OSSF order.
- **Income from common assets.** Leasing the common tract for grazing or bees helps the ag qualification ([[Agricultural Valuation]]) and pays a little. The barndominium as a rental after the build covers most of the association dues.
- **Surplus and salvage.** Builder-surplus auctions, scratch-and-dent appliances and the Habitat ReStore are good for $5,000 to $10,000 a house on fixtures, doors, cabinets and tile **[J]**.
- **Single-close construction-to-permanent loans.** One set of closing costs instead of two, about $3,000 to $6,000 **[J]**.

## Not levers

- The 20% appraisal cap on non-homestead real property (Tax Code 23.231) expires 31 Dec 2026 and was not renewed in the 2025 session **[S57]**, so it will not shelter the LLC's land during development.
- USDA's Section 523 mutual self-help housing program is this exact model, group sweat-equity builds, but it runs through nonprofits for low-income buyers and will not fit the group **[J]**.
