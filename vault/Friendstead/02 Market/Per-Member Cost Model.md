# Per-Member Cost Model

In the east-side base case the cooperative self-build lands at **$650,000 to $695,000 per household**, depending on whether each household delivers about 3,000 or 1,500 labor hours, against **$770,000** for the same home built alone on a bought lot: a saving of $75,000 to $120,000, or 10 to 16% **[M]**. Without sweat equity the cooperative costs more than going alone, because the barndominium and shared road add cost that the land discount does not cover. Part of [[Friendstead]]; inputs in [[Austin Land and Home Market]]; assumptions in [[Friendstead Open Questions]].

![[friendstead-cost-by-path.png]]

The home segment is where the difference lives: $290,000 owner-built with member labor against $450,000 contractor-built. The land and shared-development segments together cost the co-op more than the solo lot, because the barndominium and a county-standard road are extra assets, not savings.

## How the base case is built

| Line | Co-op self-build, 3,000 hours | Co-op self-build, 1,500 hours | Co-op, contractor-built homes | Solo lot, contractor-built | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Land | $140,000 | $140,000 | $140,000 | $200,000 | 30 acres at $28,000 an acre split six ways, versus a 5-acre lot at $40,000 an acre **[S2]** |
| Shared development | $115,000 | $115,000 | $115,000 | $0 | Road $150,000 paved, barndo $250,000 plus $25,000 septic, electric backbone $60,000, engineering and plat $80,000, legal $50,000, 10% contingency; split six ways **[M]** |
| Lot utilities | $50,000 | $50,000 | $50,000 | $60,000 | Well $18,000, aerobic septic $18,000, electric drop $8,000, driveway $6,000 **[S5]** |
| Home, 2,000 sq ft stick-built | $290,000 | $335,000 | $450,000 | $450,000 | Contractor at $225 per sq ft all-in **[S4]**; the co-op removes the 18% fee and overhead, then members displace up to $90,000 of labor in framing and finishes **[S6]** |
| Soft costs and contingency | $30,000 | $30,000 | $35,000 | $35,000 | Plans, permits, builder's risk, 5 to 8% contingency |
| Financing during build | $25,000 | $25,000 | $25,000 | $25,000 | Interest on a 35% down land loan and construction draws over 18 months |
| **Total cash** | **$650,000** | **$695,000** | **$815,000** | **$770,000** | |
| Member labor | about 3,000 hours | 1,500 hours | 0 | 0 | Not cash; the saving works out to $40 to $80 an hour |

The metro median home at $421,000 **[S1]** is cheaper than every build path, but it is an existing house on a subdivision lot.

## Land share by price tier

East side at $28,000 an acre with the 10-acre common tract split equally **[M]**:

| Tier | Lot | Lot cost | Common-tract share | Land total | Household total at 1,500 hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 2 acres | $56,000 | $47,000 | $103,000 | $658,000 |
| B | 3 acres | $84,000 | $47,000 | $131,000 | $686,000 |
| C | 5 acres | $140,000 | $47,000 | $187,000 | $742,000 |

A smaller or simpler home moves a tier more than the lot does: a 1,600 sq ft barndominium on a 2-acre lot comes in near $540,000, the floor for this project on the east side. See [[Build Systems Compared]].

## What the barndominium is really buying

The shared building costs each household about $46,000. Six households renting during an 18-month build at $1,800 a month would spend about $194,000 in total, or $32,000 each, so the barndo nearly pays for itself in avoided rent and then remains as a workshop and community building **[M]**. If the group will not live on site during construction, drop it to a $100,000 shell and the per-household total falls by about $25,000.

## Sensitivity

| Change | Effect per household |
|---|---|
| Land $10,000 an acre higher | +$50,000 |
| Hill Country tract at $60,000 an acre, limestone well and rock septic | +$170,000 (co-op about $820,000; solo about $930,000; saving holds near $110,000) |
| Home 1,600 sq ft instead of 2,000 | about −$60,000 |
| Five households instead of six | +$25,000 land and shared costs each |
| Gravel road accepted by the county | −$15,000 |
| Three households on 11-acre lots, no plat | land +$30,000 and shared costs +$40,000 each, but no plat, engineering or county road standard; net about +$40,000 with far less process risk |
| Members do only cleanup and painting | saving shrinks to about $40,000 |
