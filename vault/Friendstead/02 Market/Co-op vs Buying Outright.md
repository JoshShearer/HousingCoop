# Co-op vs Buying Outright

The cooperative wins on land cost, build cost and the kind of property it produces; it loses on time, liquidity, financing simplicity and dependence on other people **[J]**. Part of [[Friendstead]]; numbers from [[Per-Member Cost Model]].

| Dimension | Cooperative self-build | Buy a finished home | Solo lot and contractor build |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash cost, base case | $650,000 to $695,000 for a new 2,000 sq ft stick-built home on 2 to 5 acres with a shared workshop; $70,000 to $110,000 less as a barndominium | $421,000 metro median for an existing house on a small lot; $340,000 in Bastrop **[S1]** | About $770,000 for the same home on a 5-acre lot |
| Land per dollar | Best: one 30-acre purchase at $28,000 an acre | Worst: a subdivision lot | Middle: 5-acre lots carry a 40% per-acre premium |
| Time to move in | 24 to 36 months from contract, with 12 to 18 of them living in the barndo | 30 to 60 days | 12 to 18 months after lot closing |
| Financing | Hardest: land loan at 20 to 50% down to the LLC, then individual construction loans only after each lot is deeded; a Texas lender will not let the homeowner be the contractor on homestead property **[S41]** | Easiest: a conventional mortgage | Moderate: lot loan then construction-to-permanent |
| Homestead and property tax | Only after the deed; none while the LLC holds title | Immediate | After closing on the lot |
| Resale and liquidity | Lowest: right of first refusal, POA covenants, a buyer pool that wants a six-household community | Highest | High |
| Risk of loss | Feasibility, overruns, a member default, an injury on a work crew, the group falling apart | Market risk only | Builder default, overruns |
| Quality control | Depends on member skill; licensed trades still professional | Inspect and negotiate | Builder's warranty |
| Personal relationships | The biggest hidden cost and the biggest benefit | None | None |
| Shared assets | Workshop, equipment barn, road and well at one-sixth cost | None | None |
| Exit before completion | Formula buyout at a discount, cash not land | Sell | Sell the lot |

Two things the table cannot show. The sweat-equity saving is only real if members actually deliver the hours over 18 months while holding jobs; a household that can give 500 hours should be modeled as paying a larger share of the shared development cost instead. And the buyer's market cuts both ways: land sellers are negotiable now, but a finished home bought at 92% of list in 2026 is also a good deal, and the household that needs housing in six months should take it.
