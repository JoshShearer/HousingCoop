# Austin Land and Home Market

Tracts of 15 to 50 acres east of Austin list at roughly $20,000 to $40,000 an acre; the same size in Hays or Williamson lists at $45,000 to $65,000, and Travis higher still **[S2]**. South of Austin, Guadalupe County (Seguin, Kingsbury) is the one area cheaper than the east side at $15,000 to $28,000 an acre, while Comal County (New Braunfels, Canyon Lake) runs $38,000 to $110,000 **[S106]**. West, the Hill Country splits: Llano and Gillespie list at $12,000 to $25,000 an acre but sit 75 miles out; Blanco and Burnet, within an hour, list at $24,000 to $55,000 **[S106]**. Finished homes are in a buyer's market: the metro median sold for $421,000 in September 2026 at 92.7% of list **[S1]**. Part of [[Friendstead]]; feeds [[Per-Member Cost Model]].

## Land

![[friendstead-land-price-by-county.png]]

Caldwell and Bastrop medians sit at a third to a half of Hays, Williamson and Travis, and even their highest listings stay under the western counties' medians. The dashed line is what large ranch tracts trade for across the region: the Austin–Waco–Hill Country regional average was **$8,040 an acre in Q2 2026**, up 8.6% on the year, on a typical tract of 199 acres; South Texas, which takes in the San Antonio side, averaged $6,225 **[S3]** **[S109]**. A 30-acre tract near Austin therefore carries a three- to ten-fold small-tract premium, and the premium grows as the tract shrinks: a 5-acre lot in eastern Williamson listed at $51,000 an acre against $41,000 to $45,000 for 10 to 19 acres nearby **[S2]**. Buying one 30-acre tract and dividing it is itself a saving over six separate 5-acre lots.

## Rules and water by county

The land price is only half the comparison. Each county sets its own minimum lot for a well plus septic, and the groundwater district decides whether a well can be drilled at all **[S110]** **[S111]** **[S112]** **[S113]**.

| County | Minimum lot, well + septic | Platting gate on water | Groundwater district, status | Well depth and installed cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bastrop (east, base) | 1 acre, average over 2 acres **[S8]** | None beyond TCEQ | Lost Pines GCD (rules not opened) | $10,000 to $25,000 **[S5]** |
| Guadalupe | 1.0 acre, with or without public water | Licensed geoscientist certifies groundwater for 30 years | Guadalupe County GCD, Carrizo–Wilcox; monitoring wells 280 to 478 ft (search only) | Sand aquifer, cheaper than limestone **[J]** |
| Comal | 1.0 acre with public water; **5.01 acres** with a well | Certification of Groundwater Availability for any plat over five lots on wells (30 TAC ch. 230); on the Edwards recharge zone, 1 acre per septic and 20% impervious cover | Comal Trinity GCD; Edwards Aquifer Authority on the recharge zone (rules not opened) | 200 to 400 ft at New Braunfels, $9,000 to $18,000; 500 to 900 ft toward Spring Branch and Canyon Lake, $18,000 to $35,000 |
| Hays west | Hays rules not opened | | **Hays Trinity GCD: Emergency stage since Sept 2024, 40% curtailment, no new non-exempt permits**; a domestic well under 25,000 gal/day is exempt from drilling and operating permits | 250 to 550 ft, $11,000 to $24,000 |
| Blanco | **5 acres** with a well, 250 ft frontage, and no more than one lot per 6 acres; 3 acres with public water | Water Availability Report with a test well and a monitor well per 100 acres, pump-tested and reviewed by the county's expert | Blanco–Pedernales GCD: **no operating permits for new wells in the Upper Trinity, Edwards–Trinity or Marble Falls aquifers**; domestic wells under 25,000 gal/day exempt | Middle Trinity, 500 to 900 ft; guide does not cover Blanco, assume $18,000 to $35,000 **[J]** |

| Gillespie | Not opened | | Hill Country UWCD (not opened) | 300 to 600 ft, $12,000 to $25,000 |
| Llano | Not opened | | No district found; granite country, Hickory and Ellenburger aquifers **[J]** | Not in the guide |

Two consequences for the design. First, six households on 5-acre lots plus a common tract for the barndominium need at least 36 acres in Comal or Blanco, not 30, which multiplies an already higher per-acre price. In Guadalupe or Burnet the 1-acre minimum leaves 30 acres with room for a seventh lot or more common land. Second, the limestone counties carry water risk the east side does not: yields in the Trinity vary from 2 to 20 gallons a minute between neighbouring lots, a dry hole costs $3,000 to $8,000, and the guide recommends budgeting 20 to 30% above the quote **[S112]**. On the Edwards recharge zone an owner building a single home is exempt from a TCEQ pollution-abatement plan at 20% impervious cover or less, but a subdivision is not (30 TAC § 213.5(b)(4)(D)(ii)(III), (h)(2)) **[S113]**.

Against the $650,000 to $695,000 per-household figure in [[Per-Member Cost Model]], Guadalupe takes 7 to 8% off, Comal or Hays west add 23 to 28%, and Blanco or Burnet add 12 to 13% before the deeper well. Property tax runs the other way in the Hill Country: Johnson City ISD at $0.8608, Blanco ISD $0.8119, Burnet CISD $0.8719 and Marble Falls ISD $0.8855 per $100 are 15 to 25 cents under Bastrop ISD's $1.0679, with county rates of $0.33 to $0.35, worth about $1,800 to $1,900 a year on a $550,000 homestead against the east-side base; Comal ISD ($1.0748) and Seguin ISD ($1.083) match Bastrop ISD, but their county rates of $0.31 to $0.33 still save about $1,000 to $1,200 **[S114]** **[M]**. The Hill Country also brings the Trinity moratoriums, the 5-acre minimums and a sub-contractor market the Cedar video priced at a premium for distance (see [[Build Systems Compared]]). On price alone, the order is Guadalupe, then the east side, then Blanco or Burnet, then Comal or western Hays; the east side keeps its place because it is the cheapest land inside 35 miles with the loosest rules and the shallowest water.
