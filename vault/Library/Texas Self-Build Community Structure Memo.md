# Texas Self-Build Community Structure Memo

What the memo says, kept faithful. Analysis and my conclusions are in [[Friendstead]] and [[Entity and Ownership Design]]. Pasted into Claude Code on 22 Oct 2026 with encoding damage; this is the substance.

## Core recommendation

Development LLC buys the tract → plat and subdivide → individually deeded homesites plus a separately owned common tract → recorded common-facility, road and utility agreement plus a mutual labor agreement. Compare against a Business Organizations Code ch. 251 cooperative before finalizing; the memo expects the LLC path to win because a co-op's one-member-one-vote, patronage economics and nonprofit character fit poorly with separately owned homes.

## Points raised

1. **Partition.** Prop. Code § 23.001 lets any co-owner compel partition of land held as tenants in common; an LLC avoids this because members own personal-property interests, not the land.
2. **Feasibility before purchase.** Platting under Loc. Gov't Code ch. 232; Travis County's 5-acre minimum for lots on individual wells; Austin ETJ tracts go through the city. Make the contract contingent on a feasibility period long enough for an engineer and land-use attorney to confirm the lot yield.
3. **Shared barndominium.** Settle the permitted occupancy and the Phase 1/2/3 ownership before building; septic sizing follows the permit.
4. **Sweat equity.** A Mutual Construction Labor Agreement with labor units per household (example: 1,500), credit rates by task, and a cash conversion for shortfalls. Assume disability, transfer, divorce, job loss, death and disengagement will happen.
   > [!warning] Superseded 2026-10-23
   > Credit rates and a cash conversion would make the exchange taxable barter. Friendstead counts labor in hours only, with no dollar price and no cash between households; see [[Entity and Ownership Design#Labor without a tax bill]].
5. **Labor is not volunteering.** Employment, workers' comp and liability exposure; insurance program (GL, WC or occupational accident, builder's risk, equipment, auto, umbrella) as part of the structure.
6. **Licensed trades stay licensed.** Electrical and plumbing have state regimes; no labor credit for unqualified work.
7. **Ten interlocking documents.** Operating agreement, subscription agreement, development plan, labor agreement, homesite allocation, common facilities declaration, use restrictions, buy-sell, ROFR, construction rules.
8. **Voting tiers.** Majority for operations; two-thirds or 75% for capital spending above a threshold; unanimity for sale, new mortgage, new members, reallocation, dissolution before plat.
9. **Capital-call ladder** with named remedies: member loan, project loan, dilution, lien, deferred balance with interest, forced buyout, reduced allocation.
10. **Parcel equalization.** Baseline homesite value with credits and debits for the better and worse lots.
11. **Exit provisions.** Voluntary, death, disability, divorce, bankruptcy, default, expulsion, project failure.
12. **Resale.** ROFR with a 30–60 day match window; recorded covenants (Prop. Code ch. 202 regulates restrictive covenants).
13. **Recorded easements** for road and utilities with maintenance allocation.
14. **Septic and water geometry** decide the layout; bring the civil engineer in before choosing the property.
15. **Western Travis constraints:** Balcones Canyonlands habitat plan, FEMA, USACE § 404, LCRA.
16. **Mechanic's lien and homestead formalities** under Prop. Code § 53.254 once members own lots.
17. **Contingency matrix** of 40–60 events as the DNA of the contracts.

## Illustrative budget in the memo

Land $900,000; road $180,000; well and water $120,000; barndo $275,000; engineering and platting $80,000.
